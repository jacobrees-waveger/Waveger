# Score rank movement, never chart presence

Waveger scores how the Chart *reorders*, not which Songs appear on it. A
player's Calls are about climbs, falls, debut positions and exits. "Will this
Song chart?" is never asked, and neither is "who will be number one".

## Why

Consecutive Chart Weeks are nearly identical in membership and genuinely
turbulent in ordering. Research measuring seven decades of chart data puts
rank-turbulence divergence between consecutive charts at 0.09, rising to 0.45
when weighted toward top positions, with rank-movement entropy at 0.82 — the
authors' phrasing is that top positions "are dominated more by rank reshuffling
than by turnover". Separately, a public prediction market priced next week's
number one at **98%**.

So a game scored on presence or on the top spot is a lookup with one obvious
answer every week. All the available skill is in ordering, and that is the only
thing worth putting points on.

## Consequences

**Lock must fall before the free midweek chart update publishes** (Monday
17:45 UK). It previews the same week's result and is roughly 90% accurate on
composition; if Slips are still open after it, Waveger is a
reading-comprehension test.

Points scale with how unlikely a Call was, so the pricing of unlikeliness is
the core scoring mechanic rather than a refinement.

Two things deliberately **not** built: ownership-differential scoring, which no
major fantasy game uses despite herding being their worst problem; and scoring
on raw magnitude, which is what allowed public solvers to completely solve
Fantasy Movie League.
